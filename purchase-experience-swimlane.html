Omnichannel → Branch Companion

The digital platform's role shifts as you move up tiers. Marcus: "Branch and digital are the same record." Philip: "Platform supports what happens in the branch relationship — especially for commercial work where Victor and Jake help spec unfamiliar systems." Elena: "API data layer — she may never use the UI." Diego: "Spanish-language reference guide that reinforces what the OSR showed him." The platform isn't replacing the branch — it's the persistent record that keeps everyone aligned.

Three-Tiered Intelligence

Layer 1: Branch defaults — curated templates, promotional deals, high stock availability. This is all Diego uses. Layer 2: Explicit contractor preferences — Marcus's saved templates, Philip's office-manager-maintained library. Layer 3: AI-driven recommendations from order history — waste factor calibration, product suggestions, margin optimization. Builds automatically over time. All three layers operate within CPQ’s configuration validity and pricing constraint rules — the governing constraint space that ensures recommendations never break warranty eligibility, compatibility requirements, or pricing guardrails.

ATB (Address-to-Bid)

The product that bundles measurement → template selection → material configuration → proposal formatting → send into one estimation step. Powers Path A for Marcus, Philip, and Elena. Skips Template & Build (Stage 4) and Quoting (Stage 5). ATB is residential-only — commercial jobs require custom estimation through the branch.

Multi-Path Ordering Model

Marcus, Philip, and Elena each have three ordering paths: Path A (ATB Proposal) — fastest path from lead to bid, residential. Path B (Standard Order) — most common day-to-day, template-driven procurement at profile pricing. Path C (Special/Custom Pricing) — branch-involved, commercial-capable, iterative quoting. Philip's Path C is unique: all four stages active (core, core, core, core) because commercial quoting requires estimation, build, quoting, and acceptance separately.

Job Entity

The persistent record anchoring every estimate, order, delivery, and invoice for a project. Created once, visible across all touchpoints (branch counter, phone app, website) instantly. The omnichannel backbone.

CPQ (Configure, Price, Quote)

A rules evaluation layer — not a separate ordering flow — that validates every basket before it becomes a proposal or order. CPQ enforces configuration validity (warranty eligibility, required companions, compatibility), pricing constraints (margin floors, discount authorization), generates internal quote documents, and manages approval workflows when constraints are violated. CPQ is invisible on simple orders and progressively reveals guardrails as basket complexity increases. On ecommerce paths (ATB Blue, Builds), CPQ surfaces advisory and soft interventions during the Build flow — before the proposal goes out. On POS/branch paths, CPQ adds hard enforcements including pricing floor gates and approval routing. CPQ’s evaluation state travels with the basket across channels: if a contractor starts a Build on their phone and Victor modifies it at the branch, CPQ sees the full history. The rules are channel-agnostic; the UX response is channel-specific.

Growth Arcs by Persona

Each persona follows a distinct growth trajectory. The platform’s role shifts from capability enabler to intelligence layer to operational infrastructure depending on where the contractor is in their arc.

Diego — Capability Unlock

Subcontractor → full-scale business owner. Delivery enables larger jobs. Professional Spanish-language proposals give him credibility he’s never had. Cash → trust-first credit after dozens of successful transactions. The platform teaches him to be a profitable business — margin visibility, waste tracking, and business intelligence in his language for the first time.

Marcus — Passive Intelligence

Solo operator → data-informed business. Waste calibration improves with every job. Margin visibility reveals which jobs are actually profitable. The platform learns his patterns and gets smarter around him — he doesn’t actively manage it, but the intelligence compounds over time.

Philip — Growth Partner

Residential-only → commercial-capable. The branch helps him learn systems he doesn’t have templates for. The platform surfaces commercial opportunities based on his margin data and job history. Crew performance tracking, residential vs. commercial profitability split, and CRM integration make QXO a growth partner, not just a supplier.

Elena — Integration Depth

Not a growth arc — a depth arc. ServiceTitan sync, NetSuite invoicing, delivery API, template library. Integration stickiness compounds with every closed job. She’s not growing toward something new; she’s becoming operationally dependent on what already works. The cost of unwinding is what makes the relationship durable.

Spanish-First Experience

The Hispanic contractor base is systematically underserved by English-only distribution. Platform UI, proposals, invoices, job records, AI chatbot for product specs — all need Spanish-language support. Spanish-language distribution is a direct market entry strategy. Diego is 70% more likely to buy at retail not because of price but because nobody has built the distributor experience for him in his language.

Cash-Centric Purchase Flow

QXO has historically pushed every customer onto credit accounts with credit lines. For the emerging contractor segment, this is a barrier, not a feature. The opportunity: design the purchase experience as cash-first — no credit application pop-ups, no upsell to net-30, just clear invoicing in Spanish. Introduce credit only after trust is built through dozens of successful cash transactions, from the OSR, backed by job history data. "You've done $180K in jobs this year. A credit line means you don't have to front $8K for the next big job."

WhatsApp as Ordering Channel

Diego texts his OSR through WhatsApp for everything — leads, quotes, supplemental orders. These conversations happen outside the system entirely. The flywheel opportunity: capture WhatsApp messages tied to the OSR, recognize the Job and materials, generate a supplemental order for the rep to confirm. The communication channel Diego already uses becomes the ordering channel. Low stakes, urgent need, clear outcome, in his language.

AI Chatbot in Spanish

Most product docs and specifications are English-only. A Spanish-language AI chatbot that translates catalog questions on the fly would be a sticky feature: "What underlayment do I need for this pitch?" answered instantly in Spanish. This bridges the knowledge gap between Diego's installation expertise and the distributor catalog system he's never been trained on.
