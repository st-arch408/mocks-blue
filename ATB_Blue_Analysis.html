Executive Summary

  • Prior contract year (Aug 2024–Aug 2025): 103,693 reports, $4,178,349.18 total spend — exceeded $4M guarantee by 4.5%

  • Material sales are the margin: At $4,500 AOV and 22% gross margin, each upgraded report generates ~$990 in material gross margin — making the ~$25.58 upgrade cost a ~39x return as a Customer Acquisition Cost

4. Summary: Is $20 the Right Price?

Arguments FOR $20 Flat Rate

  1. Material sales make it a no-brainer: At $4,500 AOV and 22% margin, each upgraded report generates ~$990.00 in material gross margin — making the $25.58 upgrade cost a 39x return
  2. Competitive with BidPerfect at $18 — only $2 more, but includes free premium upgrades (massive perceived value)
  3. Simplicity wins: eliminates confusing tiered pricing across 34 products
  4. Drives guaranteed spend: each ATB Blue report generates $40.33 avg in EagleView spend, helping close the $0.7M gap
  5. Guaranteed material conversion: Premium upgrade gives contractor a full takeoff — they NEED materials to complete the job, creating a captive purchase moment
  6. Entry-level price point is below the $40.89 blended avg, maximizing volume and material sales opportunities

Arguments FOR a Higher Price ($22–$25)

  1. Report-fee margin buffer: At $22, report-only net margin at 50% upgrade = $9.21/report vs $7.21 at $20
  2. $25 is margin-safe on fee alone: Positive even at 75% upgrade rate without counting material sales
  3. Price anchoring: Customers anchor to current $41–$66 premium pricing; $20 may leave money on the table
  4. However: Every $1 increase in ATB price reduces volume and therefore reduces guaranteed material sales — the material margin ($990.00/sale) dwarfs any per-report fee increase

Recommendation

$20 is the optimal price point. When material sales are factored in:

  • The upgrade cost ($25.58 avg) is not a subsidy — it's a $25.58 CAC that yields $990.00 in immediate material gross margin (39x return)
  • Even at 100% upgrade rate + only 25% material conversion, net value per report is $241.92 — far above any higher price point without material sales
  • Maximizing volume at $20 (vs $25) drives more upgrades → more material sales → more gross margin — the marginal value of each additional report is ~$371.25 (50% upgrade, 75% conversion)
  • $20 also helps close the $735,956.80 contract guarantee gap while generating $369,000.00 in rebate credit

Bottom line: ATB Blue at $20 isn’t a discount product — it’s a material sales funnel. The report fee is the loss leader; the roofing materials are the business.

Appendix C: Methodology Notes

  • Data cleaning: Removed trailing summary/total rows from all sheets. Filtered to valid posting dates only.
  • Rpt Cnt = 0 rows: Delivery and fee companion rows have Rpt Cnt = 0 and are excluded from report volume counts but included in revenue totals.
  • EC Premium Residential: Contract has 3 tiers ($20.50 Small, $41.50 Medium, $66 Large). Data does not distinguish tiers; blended avg of ~$40.89 is used.
  • Account normalization: 'Beacon' and 'Beacon Customer Pro Plus' in the rebate data are mapped to 'QXO' and 'QXO Customer Pro Plus' respectively (corporate rebrand).
  • Upgrade cost model: Assumes QXO pays EagleView the contract price for each upgrade product and collects $20 from the customer. The delta is the subsidy/upgrade cost.
  • Material sales model: QXO online AOV of $4,500 used as primary material revenue assumption. Full takeoff material cost independently estimated at ~$4,200 (30 squares × $140/square all-in), validating the AOV figure.
  • Distributor gross margin: Modeled at 20% (conservative), 22% (mid), and 25% (aggressive). 22% used as primary scenario for roofing distribution.
  • Material sale conversion: Not all upgraded reports convert to material purchases. Primary projections use 75% conversion rate; sensitivity analysis covers 25–100%.
  • Revenue projections: Current contract monthly rate calculated from Aug 15 2025 through Feb 25 2026 data, annualized to 12 months. Seasonal adjustments use prior year monthly patterns.
  • Rebate credit: 9% of actual spend, credit-only (not cash), expires 6 months after contract term.
